## Common patterns noticed

1. Booking is nearly always outsourced to a platform (Fresha, Treatwell, Planity, Phorest, Zenoti, Square, Booker) behind a single persistent "Book" button; own-site booking is rare. The template should offer a button/widget slot per location.
2. Multi-site brands (Buff, Nailology, Art of Nails, Parlour Room, MINK, Pure) use a location picker that leads to a per-location booking link, address block and hours.
3. Palettes split into three camps: warm neutral luxury (white, beige/taupe, charcoal, gold or deep green accent), dark glam (near-black, purple/raspberry), and playful bright (Shoreditch Nails, Tropical Popical). Serif display (Cormorant, Italiana, Playfair, Libre Baskerville, Ivy Presto) over a plain sans body is the dominant luxury pairing.
4. Heroes are image-led: close-up hands/nail art or interior, one clear CTA, tagline of 3-8 words. Galleries of recent work show up on the home page (Feather, From M, Horni).
5. Price menus appear as tiered or from-price lists: tiers by time (Townhouse), BIAB/gel/extension rows with from-prices (Em, Mitty, Milano), nail art priced in levels (Mitty, Milano). Several sites hide prices behind the booking widget, which reduces trust compared with a visible menu.
6. Specialist keywords carried in headlines and meta titles: BIAB (UK, IE, NL, AU), Russian manicure (FR, ES, NZ, AU), Gel-X / softgel (ES, NL, AU), Japanese gel (CA), vegan / free-from (AU, UK, FR).
7. Trust and retention elements recur: review counts (4.8 to 5.0 with large totals), gift vouchers (nearly every site), loyalty or membership (Mitty points, Art of Nails members, MINK treat card, Shoreditch club, Manucurist Green Addict), cancellation policy text, hygiene claims.
8. Extra revenue lines beside services: own polish/care shop (Townhouse, Manucurist, Buff, MINK, Shoreditch Nails), academy/training (Shoreditch Nails, Mitty, Art of Nails), parties/groups/bridal (Tropical Popical, MINK, Nailology, Parlour Room), and a blog/journal for SEO (Tropical Popical, Mitty, Parlour Room, Iris Avenue).
9. Hospitality and experience framing beats technique alone: coffee bar (Nailology), cocktails/welcome drink (Iris Avenue), prosecco and tea (Nails and Beauty Firenze), "luxurious, cozy and minimalistic" (Em).
10. Small independents in Germany, Italy and the Netherlands rely on simple templates with phone/WhatsApp contact and Treatwell links; gaps worth filling in a template are a visible price list, a gallery, directions, and a bilingual layout (many serve English and Russian speakers).
